## Conclusions per question
**(a) Supervised, user-present browser assistance (Claude in Chrome, human approves every generation): GREY, leaning
permitted.** MEDIUM confidence. No fetched official page forbids a user operating Flow in their own browser with an
assistant; T1 bars automated access only "in violation of the machine-readable instructions" (robots-style rules), and
nothing read bars UI assistance. No Flow page documents or endorses it, and no page mentions bots/automation for Flow
(T9 contains nothing on it). Residual risk: account-level action on the Google account holding the AI Pro plan
(INFERRED). Mitigations in the workflow: visible browser, human present, one approved request at a time, human pace,
stop on any CAPTCHA/login/safety prompt, no masking, no account switching.

**(b) Unattended automation (loops, schedulers, headless, bursts): treat as PROHIBITED for this project.** Not
explicitly forbidden by a quote we read, but it falls under T1/T6 spirit (abuse protections; Flow help warns of limits for
"suspicious activity or very rapid generations", D-feas §1.2), is outside the supervised envelope the user approved
(D015), and unreviewed third-party Flow tools remain rejected (D013). Decision rule: if the user is not watching, do not run.

**(c) Commercial use of outputs: UNRESOLVED, leaning permitted for Google AI Pro with caveats.** MEDIUM-LOW confidence.
Official text says only that the Terms of Service govern use (T9) and "Google won't claim ownership" (T2, T10); no read
page grants an explicit commercial licence for Flow images/video (explicit "Commercial use rights" is listed for Flow Music
only, T15). A community thread claims Labs tools are non-commercial "Preview" products; that is a **non-official** post
and could not be confirmed (T17: Labs terms page unreadable). Also: no claim of exclusive copyright over raw AI output is
made by any source, and AI output may be uncopyrightable in some jurisdictions (INFERRED, general knowledge, unverified here).
Needed before publishing: the user reads the live Flow Terms/Labs terms in their account (or asks Google support) and we
record the answer. Until then, treat outputs as "development and review use"; do not publish as final.
Related: T3 forbids using outputs to train/develop ML models, so Flow images must never feed model training or
fine-tuning datasets (note for A21/A24 pipelines: no LoRA/training on Flow output).

**(d) Watermark and credential duties: PRESERVE; never remove.** HIGH confidence for the rule we adopt. T12 says the
SynthID watermark "should not be tampered with or removed"; T13 says altering C2PA history without update raises an
error; T4/T7 forbid deceiving others that generated content is human-made. Our rule: keep downloaded originals
byte-exact (including the `caBX` chunk and visible mark) in private storage; no watermark removal/inpainting; any derived
asset (cut-out, cropped part) is recorded as derived, and published videos must carry honest AI disclosure per platform
rules (decision for the user; the visible mark will be absent in derived cut-outs, which is not a licence to claim human
authorship). The visible watermark is required in the user's region (locked on), so cut-outs for the final film need
a user decision on how to handle it (open question Q3). A derived crop that removes the visible sparkle edits the content,
so it must be a user-approved, recorded decision, not a pipeline default.

## Privacy note (T14)
Do not put confidential material, personal data or unreleased scripts into Flow prompts or uploads; reviewers can read
interactions for up to 18 months unless "Help improve Google Flow" is turned off (user setting; ask the user).

## Limits of this review
Summaries came from a fetch-and-summarise model, not a raw read; labs.google/terms unreadable; no Flow-specific
automation clause found (absence of evidence); regional terms differ (user region requires visible watermark); terms
effective 2026-07-30 may change: re-check at each phase start.
