# A17 TECHNIQUES — story sourcing (derived from F-a17-001…009)

## 1. Sourcing workflow
1. Brainstorm 40–60 candidate seeds from the topic areas in §4 (a seed is a real event, person, object or mechanism,
   not a title).
2. Originality gate (before research): compare with the 30 RC30 titles and F-a17-001 categories; reject if the same
   event/object/story appears (spear-shield, space toilet, Zhang Heng, pachinko, Baker-Miller, Srivastava, grave bell,
   clothoid loops, mine salting are taken). A different story in a shared category is allowed.
3. Quick triage (15 min/seed): find 2 independent sources; drop seeds with only a popular-press source.
4. Score with the rubric (§2); keep seeds ≥ 70 with all gates passed.
5. Full dossier (§3) for the top 3–5; search explicitly for debunkings ("myth", "fact check", "origin", "no evidence").
6. Claim ledger: one row per factual narration line; status verified / simplified / contested / unverifiable;
   unverifiable lines removed or reworded ("legend says…") before voice recording.
7. Hand-offs: dossier → A18; top surprising verified facts → A19; final ledger → A29.

## 2. Selection rubric (100 points + gates)
| Criterion | Pts | 0 | Full marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visual mechanism (camera can show the answer) | 25 | abstract rule | one object/space process animatable in ≤ 3 shots, readable sound-off |
| Surprise / curiosity gap | 20 | answer guessable from title | counter-intuitive mechanism arriving at 55–75 % runtime |
| Stakes | 10 | none | money, life, status or war on the line |
| Verifiability | 25 | popular-press only | ≥ 2 independent sources incl. one primary/scholarly; no credible debunking |
| Originality vs RC30 | 15 | same story | no overlap in story, object or protagonist |
| Production fit | 5 | many sets | ≤ 6 sets, ≤ 4 characters, 45–65 s |
Gates (fail = reject): originality overlap with an RC30 title; verifiability < 15; known myth unless the story is the
debunking; needs real people's likeness or restricted artwork. Tie-break: a mid-story "Wait…" reframe (F-a19-007) and
a prop that can carry the gap as readable text. Weights untested against retention (HYPOTHESIS).

## 3. Dossier template (`productions/<id>/research/DOSSIER.md`)
Header (title, one-line claim, date, researcher, source count); logline (own words) and category; timeline (date,
event, source ID); mechanism in 3 sentences + what can be shown; sources table (ID, exact URL, access date, type:
primary / peer-reviewed / reference / news / popular, independence note) — each key claim ≥ 2 independent sources, one
primary/scholarly where possible; Wikipedia and content farms are leads, never sources; claims table (`CLAIMS.md`:
claim | sources | status | safe wording | line #); myths/contested points with both evidence sets and wording true
under either; top-5 surprising verified facts for A19; originality check vs the 30 RC30 titles (result, date); open
questions and exclusions.

## 4. Topic areas NOT in RC30 (brainstorm leads, none verified)
Aviation accident investigation with a physical cause; structural/bridge failures and the fix; telegraph/radio/cable
history (avoid 12's wire con); standards and measurement (time zones, thread standards, gauge lock-in — avoid 10);
economics oddities (currency, perverse bounties); animal behaviour and ecology; food processing/preservation;
textiles and manufacturing history; cryptography flaws; cartography/navigation instruments (avoid 11); fire safety and
building codes; electricity/engineering rivalries; acoustics and architecture (whispering galleries); sports rules
loopholes and equipment (not gambling); medicine history other than painkillers (anaesthesia, hygiene, vaccination
method); geology/volcano/flood-defence engineering; printing and postal systems; polar/expedition logistics;
ocean/deep-sea engineering; legal oddities outside Japan; space history other than toilets.

## 5. Wording rules for narration
Use "records say", "according to", "claimed" for legend-grade facts; avoid single-inventor attributions when sources
disagree; avoid exact percentages when sources give ranges.
